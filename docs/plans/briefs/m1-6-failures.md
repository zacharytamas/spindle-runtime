## 1. Points of failure in execution order

The highest-consequence cuts are **12** (an external action with no receipt), **05** (a live old tool and a new owner), and **08** (acknowledged work exists only in RAM). Use the numbers to run the following walk in order.

1. **Accept send.** Simultaneous sends each believe they started the thread. An input can commit before its wake, or the sender can die before receiving its receipt.
2. **Accept queued work.** A locked or full database rejects a write after the caller was told it succeeded. An invalid or unauthorized tool is admitted.
3. **Persist queued user-code output.** A hook or tool copies a secret into a queued payload or failure diagnostic before ordinary message redaction runs.
4. **Claim.** Kill between status and holder writes, or race two claims from stale idle reads. A rolling upgrade can also encounter old messages and status with no claim.
5. **Renew.** A long tool, paused process or busy renewal misses the deadline. A late renewal, write or release from its old flow can displace the successor.
6. **Measure silence.** A wall-clock jump expires healthy holders or leaves dead ones apparently alive indefinitely.
7. **Order and route the drain.** Concurrent producers, equal timestamps, mixed entry kinds, silent records or reversed sides confuse delivery.
8. **Transfer out of the inbox.** The batch disappears before delivery commits, or remains eligible after delivery. Later entries disappear when the first one's run fails.
9. **Process the head.** One accepted entry becomes unprocessable after admission. It can poison every future drain, or silently disappear.
10. **Assemble.** An independent handle commits an insert, update or delete between drain, filter snapshot and dispatch. The cached history can miss it or mutate a dispatched request.
11. **Call model and persist response.** A truncated stream exposes a partial call, a retry carries it forward, or a failed response write still dispatches tools.
12. **Execute tools and persist each result.** Kill before dispatch, after an outside action, or after a result commit. Retrying the whole step repeats an effect or pairs a result with the wrong turn.
13. **Run hooks around a write.** A malformed or timed-out hook vetoes an essential result or its late continuation injects into settled history.
14. **Evaluate stop and settle.** Final empty-drain races a new admission. No process owns the newly accepted work.
15. **Release claim.** Signal or storage failure during settlement leaves a half-transition. A delayed finalizer releases a fresh claim.
16. **Recover.** Two observers repair the same call twice or a stale scan repairs a newer run. A second crash interrupts repair.
17. **Read settled status.** A read, renewal or queued input resets the reported age, or the CLI substitutes a display label for the persisted reason.
18. **Stop during model call.** The returning response has tool calls. Aborting the model, running the tools, or storing bare calls gives different failures.
19. **Stop during tool.** A hanging tool ignores the signal. An interrupted waiter releases ownership or runs a sibling; its late result overwrites cancellation.
20. **Terminate during create.** A hook's nested write commits while its outer write is pending. A stale continuation can append after shutdown.
21. **Terminate vs admission and recovery.** Another process admits work through a stale handle, or recovery resurrects a terminal thread after a crash.
22. **Repeat terminal notification.** Duplicate terminate calls misreport success/failure although the first terminal marker and all data remain correct.

## 2. Failure matrix

Run against a real shared WAL file, with two OS processes and a third independent reader. Each row describes the required **committed** view, including permitted alternatives when the caller never saw an acknowledgement. At each named gate use an IPC barrier with entered/release acknowledgements. For a transaction cut, instrument the store or test SQLite driver immediately before COMMIT and immediately after successful COMMIT before control returns. Kill the worker at that gate, reopen the file, and also inspect from the already-open observer in a fresh read transaction. For BUSY hold a separate write transaction until the tested operation reports contention; for FULL fault the named write/commit in the test driver and then restore space. Test both rollback and successful-commit/lost-reply paths. Deadlines bound the harness; sleeps establish no ordering.

For every row capture call returns, throws/rejections or killed/disconnected callers; log provider requests at entry and exit, tool entry/effect/return and flow IDs outside the worker. Compare ordered queue, full history including silent rows, call owner/ID/name/result, revision, termination, status triple and claim from one DB snapshot per checkpoint. Use identity filters and eligible uncapped history unless testing filtering. Drive surviving work to a known barrier or completed follow-up send before asserting absence of a duplicate. Use a second unrelated thread as a control. “Lost” means failed/execution_lost, before any subsequent deliberate run. “Error partner” means a stored error tool result referencing the correct call on its actual owning message, never a fabricated success.

## 3. Combined failures, step by step

1. **Y acted before its result was saved (12).** (1) Return X,Y,Z in one response, gate Y after it appends a receipt to an independent ledger. (2) Confirm through another connection the owning message and X's result are already committed. Kill without releasing Y. (3) Recover and compare X byte for byte. Count one error partner apiece for Y and Z under the same owning turn; Z's ledger count stays zero. (4) Send again. The provider sees a valid prefix including the uncertainty, Y's ledger still has one receipt. A lost result says the execution was lost; it cannot certify the outside action failed.
2. **Expired A is still executing user code (05).** (1) Gate A's tool after entry but before an external effect; suspend renewals or pause A, then advance elapsed silence. (2) Let B classify A as lost and request a new run. Observe whether B's tool enters before A exits. (3) Resume A and let it try an effect, queue, result write, renewal and settlement. Compare B's claim, messages and status before/after. Rejecting all stale DB actions protects the file but cannot prevent an arbitrary external effect. (4) If B actually executes while A's tool is live, report a blocker under the stated physical gate even with pristine SQL. If B waits, release A and verify progress. A silent paused process and a dead one can be indistinguishable to the timeout observer. The two promises need a lead ruling (§5).
3. **Two recovery processes, one dead holder (16).** (1) Both observers read the same dead holder with one orphan, one paired call and two pending messages. (2) Admit B's repair, observe lost, then hold its next deliberately triggered run inside the provider. (3) Release C's stale recovery decision. Its call may report no work or the current state. It must neither add an error, change B's since, release B, consume pending work twice nor dispatch. (4) Repeat with B killed mid-repair; C must finish only outstanding repairs. Compare call pairs, queue order, other live threads and the provider ledger.
4. **Terminate halfway through create (20).** (1) Inside before-create for an outer result, commit one nested silent injection and pause before returning. Confirm the nested ID independently. (2) Terminate from B. Record the actual terminal commit ordering, rather than using when the call started. (3) Release the hook. If termination won, the nested commit survives, outer stale write cannot land, and the committed parent call needs one truthful cancellation partner. If outer insert won, preserve that exact result. (4) Repeat in after-create and with a hook-held nested write already submitted but paused before insert. Late rejection alone proves nothing if the write committed. Check contents, revisions and subsequent rejected sends.
5. **Drained entry, then failed run (08).** (1) Accept M,Q,N, then block just after M becomes history. Inject a permanent provider failure in the next eligible model call; separately fail during delivery of Q before N is processed. (2) Observe failed with a step reason for an ordinary error, lost only for a dead executor. Identify the durable frontier: delivered M stays once; committed Q never reruns; pending Q or N retains its position. (3) Trigger a fresh run. Require complete Q pairing before provider use, order M,Q,N once across history and inbox. Seeing M again as *context* is expected; another M insert or Q effect is a duplicate.
6. **Stop while model returns tool calls (18).** (1) Obtain stop from the active execution; hold the model after dispatch and call stop. Observe its immediate void return; keep the transport alive. (2) Finish with two named calls. No next tool may begin. If the response is stored, preserve its IDs and append terminal error partners before future provider use. (3) Check idle/stop reason, null termination marker and zero tool counters, including after a fresh send. Dropping an already committed response to make pairing pass loses data. Distinguish an uncommitted response policy from that defect.

## 4. Release gate

**Block (20 rows)** when the witness demonstrates the indicated outcome. Each number maps to exactly one row.

- **Duplicate effects or overlapping executions:** 12, 05, 16, 04, 06. A new holder does not establish that old user code stopped.
- **Lost, changed, misdelivered or stranded durable input:** 08, 01, 02, 07, 09, 14. Count tokens across both pending and delivered representations. Queued work that stays pending forever on a live eligible thread cannot pass.
- **Secret disclosure:** 03. Inspect the undrained payload as well as output after redaction.
- **Corrupt history, missing partners, unauthorized late writes or unusable thread:** 20, 10, 11, 13, 15, 18, 19, 21. For 10, the blocking witness is a committed edit lost to subsequent live history/delivery or a stale view written back. For 18, it is an orphaned call, lost committed response or non-reusable thread.

**Does not block by this gate (2 rows):** 17 (display-only mismatch), 22 (repeat-call outcome only). Record conformance failures even where the release gate is narrower. A cleanly rejected busy write, temporary contention, or an external effect known only to be indeterminate is a valid tested outcome, not itself a finding. Reclassify by the actual consequence if a diagnostic defect masks a changed DB value or leaks a secret.

## 5. Decisions needed from the lead

1. **May a new flow enter user code before the old tool has demonstrably ceased?** Options are to delay new execution until cessation, or define exclusivity as ownership of durable transitions and accept overlapping external work after expiry. Recommend the former under R:239 and the stated gate. Keep the configured silence threshold for loss classification. If only silence can be observed, immediate takeover, physical exclusion and guaranteed progress cannot all be promised. Record which guarantee yields for an indefinitely paused owner.
2. **What persisted account closes calls on intentional stop/termination?** For a response returned after stop, discard it before persistence or persist it with explicit cancellation errors for each unexecuted call. Recommend the latter to preserve the completed model operation (R:47; E:63–70). For already committed calls retain the owner and pair all unfinished calls; distinguish cancellation/unknown outcome from execution_lost. Decide whether ordinary stop signals the current tool or awaits natural completion; recommend cooperative signalling for tools, leaving in-flight model calls alone.
3. **How does an operator resolve an unreadable accepted entry?** Options are retain it at the head until manually repaired, or retain its payload and sequence as a visible failed delivery and allow later work to continue. Recommend the latter, with authorization/redaction preserved; a decodable unknown tool can receive an ordinary error partner. R:221–225 and T:258 do not specify malformed durable input.
4. **How does a caller reconcile an admission whose reply was lost?** Options are a caller-correlatable durable receipt or an explicitly indeterminate result requiring inspection before resubmission. Recommend the latter within the existing D:2864,3021 signatures; never promise that a new invocation after disconnect is automatically deduplicated. For non-crash storage failures recommend synchronous rejection by throw for queueTool and promise rejection for queueMessage before acceptance.
5. **How should pending entries and status appear after termination?** Options include retained pending-but-inert entries or retained entries explicitly marked undeliverable. Recommend the latter, stable first termination time, and idle/aborted for deliberate termination of an active run; preserve the previous triple for termination at rest. R:229–232 and E:118–121 specify finality and settlement but leave these details open.
